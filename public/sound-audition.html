New: energy and resonance studies

Direction: fun space-opera action. Expressive electrical weapons, distinctive energy failures and a rewarding restoration flourish, with scale reserved for the big moments.

S5 - Space-opera action comparison (start here)Two blaster shots, reactor breakup, shield failure, then restoration. Ten seconds to compare character and scale, not a replacement soundtrack.

Approved palette: S1 cannon, S2 alien destruction, S3 shield loss and S4 shield acquisition are installed in local gameplay. S5 is the reference mix. Original designs, not movie recordings.

S1 - Resonant energy blasterDispersive electrical twang, inharmonic energy modes, mechanical trigger and low recoil.
S2 - Reactor ruptureEnergy core tearing apart, secondary electrical ruptures across stereo space, then pressure and debris.
S3 - Shield collapseResonant field failure, interrupted electrical arcs and an energy-release tail; distinct from ship destruction.
S4 - Shield restorationRising energy gathers into a spreading harmonic shimmer and a solid arrival accent. The positive side of the palette.

Cloud Defender sound palette

Shared character: weighty, physical, technological. Strong low-mid body, restrained sharpness, short early reflections; long tails reserved for destruction and major rewards. No constant maximum loudness.

Player power - precise and authoritativeA cannon: mechanical snap, energy discharge, bass recoil. Short enough to preserve rapid-fire clarity. Reference C2.
Signature: force + control
Enemy machinery - unstable and threateningMarch: mechanical pulse; bombs: distinct launch textures for malware, worm and ransomware; mystery ship: restrained moving engine presence. Destruction: hull rupture, irregular internal blasts, debris. Reference N1/N2 and C1.
Signature: tension + breakup
Firewall structure - material and groundedHits: cracks, dull impact, loose fragments. Final destruction: broader blast and cascading rubble, clearly larger than a normal hit. References C3/C4.
Signature: fracture + collapse
Shield energy - coherent and preciousLoss: strained energy tearing apart. Acquisition: gathering energy, a resolved arrival and a short landing accent synced to the HUD. Not another explosion. Still to be designed.
Signature: tension to resolution
Mission control - clear and restrainedKeep the chosen announcer; menus and wave cues use a related short tonal motif. Clear space around speech and avoid identical voice/SFX messages.
Signature: clarity + recognition

Scale: menu < march/launch < cannon/hit < alien destruction < firewall collapse and shield showcase. Small actions stay compact; major events earn width and a longer decay.

Approval order: spacecraft destruction first, then cannon beside it, firewall damage/destruction, shield events, and finally movement/UI. These audition sketches are not all approved game sounds.

New layered designs: deep blast pressure, physical impact textures, staggered breakup, stereo debris and reverberant tails. These are audition mixes, not replacements in the game yet. Start at a comfortable volume.

Design reference: Ben Burtt's physical-source approach to sci-fi sound: convincing machinery and material textures rather than only electronic beeps. No film recordings are used. These designs combine procedural audio with the credited Kenney CC0 textures below; they are not studio recordings or exact imitations.
